MOTOR INSURANCE MARKET BRIEF (FICTIONAL)
=========================================

This document is fictional and is provided only to demonstrate the Decision
Impact Simulator. Any resemblance to real companies or figures is coincidental.

Date: 2026-10-30
Source: Fictional market-intelligence brief

Market conditions
-----------------
- Average UK motor premiums rose roughly 8% across the market in the last year.
- Claims inflation remains the dominant cost pressure, estimated at 12-16%.
- Three of the five largest insurers have already announced renewal increases of
  7% to 10% for early 2027.
- Price-comparison websites now drive over 60% of new motor business, so switching
  friction is low and headline price matters more than ever.

Customer behaviour
------------------
- Roughly 1 in 3 motorists shop around at renewal; this rises sharply once an
  increase exceeds about 10%.
- Loyal customers (5+ years) churn far less, but surveys show growing resentment of
  "loyalty penalty" pricing where long-standing customers pay more than new ones.
- Low-income drivers are the most price-sensitive and the most likely to reduce
  cover or drop optional add-ons when premiums rise.

Regulatory context
------------------
- The conduct regulator has reaffirmed its fair-value rules and is scrutinising how
  increases are communicated and justified.
- Insurers are expected to show that price rises reflect genuine cost increases and
  do not unfairly target existing customers.

Implications for a 12% rise
---------------------------
- A 12% increase sits above the current market pack of 7-10%, creating a visible
  gap that competitors and the press are likely to highlight.
- Clear cost justification and targeted retention offers are seen as essential to
  limit churn and regulatory attention.
